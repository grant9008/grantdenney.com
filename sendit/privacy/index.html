SEND IT: Red Light Runner: Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 21, 2026

SEND IT: Red Light Runner ("the game") is made by Grant Thomas Denney, United States ("we"). This page says, in plain words, what the game keeps on your phone, what it sends, to whom, and how you switch it off or have it deleted.

Questions or requests: gdenns@gmail.com

1. The short version

  • You do not make an account. The game never asks for your name, email, phone number, contacts, photos or location.
  • There are no ads and nothing to buy in this version.
  • Your saved times, your settings, your recorded scream and the videos you make stay on your phone. (A copy of your best times goes to the world board only if you join it.)
  • The app asks you once, before your first ride, whether it may send anonymous play numbers (runs, crashes, finish times, frame rate and error messages) to a company called GameAnalytics, under a random id. Nothing is sent unless you say yes, and you can change your answer in the game at any time.
  • If the world board is on, the game asks you first, on a result card. Only after you tap YES does it send your best times, your three initials and how often you tried and crashed to our own rank service, under a random id, and other players see them on the world lists. You can switch it off, and erase what is online, in the game at any time.
  • We do not sell anything about you, and nothing is used to follow you across other apps or websites.

2. What stays on your phone

These never leave your phone unless you send them somewhere yourself:

  • Your progress and settings: best times, biggest bills, medals, collected golden helmets, your three leaderboard initials, how often you tried and crashed on each level, ride and traffic mode, the traffic mode you picked, your answers to the game's questions and the settings you changed. In the app a copy is also kept in the phone's own app storage, so an iOS clean-up of web data does not wipe your records. (The world board, section 4, sends a copy of your best times, initials and counts only after your YES.)
  • Your voice (the Scream Booth): if you tap RECORD, the game asks the phone for the microphone and records a few seconds. The recording is turned into the crash scream on the phone and is stored on the phone only. It is never uploaded. DELETE MINE in the Scream Booth erases it. The game does not listen at any other time.
  • Your clips: when you tap CLIPS, the game makes a video of your crash (or of other moments of your runs) on the phone. It exists only in the app until you tap SHARE. SHARE opens the phone's own share sheet; where the video goes from there (your Photos, a message, another app) is your choice, and that app's own privacy policy applies. If you use Save Video, iOS asks for permission to add to your Photos; the game can add that one video and cannot see your library.
  • The voices: the kid never talks out loud. With TALKING VOICES on, drivers and people yell with the phone's built-in voice, on the phone.

Deleting the game from your phone deletes all of this.

3. Analytics (only in the app, and only if you said yes)

To see whether the game runs well and where players get stuck, the app can report a small number of events to GameAnalytics (gameanalytics.com), a company that processes them for us.

The app asks first. The first time you open it, before your first ride, it shows the question HELP FIX THE GAME? with two answers, SURE and NO THANKS. Until you tap SURE nothing is sent, the analytics software is not even loaded, and no id is created. NO THANKS keeps it that way.

What is sent, a few events per run and nothing per frame:

  • that a run started, crashed, was given up or finished; the level, the ride and the traffic mode;
  • how far along the street, and how many seconds into the run, a crash happened and what was hit (a car, a pole, a building ...), the crash bill total;
  • a finish time, a medal or a new personal best; how many tricks were landed; how the run was ridden (time spent tucked and in SEND IT!, drafts tried and SLINGSHOTS fired and how strong they were, STOMPED IT! landings, side routes and lines taken, how many seconds into the run the first trick and the first SEND IT! came); if the app was left in the middle of a run;
  • roughly how many runs this phone has played, as a band (1, 2 to 5, 6 to 20, 21 to 100, over 100), never an exact count or a date; the band also goes with the first trick and the first SEND IT! ever landed on each level and ride on this phone (the phone keeps a short list of which of those were already sent, so each is sent once);
  • whether a collectible or an achievement was found for the first time;
  • the average frame rate of the run, and the graphics quality and kind of controls in use;
  • if the game hits an error: the one-line error message.

What GameAnalytics adds by itself: a random id it creates and keeps in the app's storage on your phone, a visit number and visit length, the game version, the operating system version, the time, and whether the phone was online. Like every internet service it receives your phone's internet (IP) address when the events arrive and uses it to work out your country.

What is never sent to GameAnalytics: your initials, your name, your voice, your videos, your contacts, a precise location, an advertising id, or anything you type.

Changing your answer: in the game, tap the gear to open SETTINGS and choose ANALYTICS: OFF (or ON). OFF stops the sending at once, throws away the events that were still waiting, and erases the random id (and the short list of firsts already sent) from your phone. If you switch it on again later, a new random id is made the next time you open the game.

The web versions of the game send no analytics at all.

4. The world board (only in a version that asks about it on a result card)

The world board shows, for each level, traffic mode and ride, the fastest players everywhere, with how often they tried and crashed. We run it ourselves, on Cloudflare (section 7). It is one list for the whole world: neither the game nor the board looks at where you are.

The game asks first. A result card asks WORLD BOARD: SHOW YOUR BEST TIMES, TRIES AND CRASHES TO OTHER PLAYERS? with YES and NO. It asks on three cards at most and never stops you from riding. Until you tap YES, nothing about you is sent: no time, no initials, no counts, no id (only a report you make or an erase, below, sends an id). You can still look at the world lists: looking sends only which list you want to see.

After YES, for each level, traffic mode and ride you play, the game sends:

  • your best official time, the same time counted in the game's steps (60 a second, so we can check that the time adds up), the three initials of that run and the game version;
  • how many tries, crashes and finishes you made there (official runs only: runs with changed gameplay settings are never counted or sent), also together with each time;
  • a random device id, made up on your phone the first time there is something to send and kept there. It is not your name and not an account: it lets a better time replace your old one and lets the game find your own row. It also goes along when the game looks up your place.

What other players see: on each list, the place, initials, best time, tries and crashes of the fastest players (the game shows the top ten and your own place), and how many players, tries and crashes the list has. A public page of the world board shows the totals of every list, with each list's fastest initials and time. Never your device id.

What the rank service keeps, per device and list: the random id, the initials, the best time and its steps, the tries, crashes and finishes, the game version and when they were set, and a private note for us when something about that time looks odd (for example, it came without counts). If the board turns a time down (faster than the level allows, over 30 minutes, or steps that do not add up to the time), it also keeps, in a private log only we can read, what that post sent: the list, the device id, the time and its steps, the initials, the game version, the counts and why it was turned down. That log keeps at most the newest 2,000 such rows, each for 30 days at most, and we may empty it at any time. It does not keep your name, an email, your IP address or any location: no country, no state, no city. To stop one network from flooding a list, it keeps a scrambled code of the network address for one day; the codes and their key are deleted every night (our host's restore history can bring a deleted day's codes back for a while: see "Deleted is not gone at once" below).

Reports. Every other player's row has a small flag, to report rude initials or an impossible time. A report sends your device id (made up at that moment if you have none), the list, and the place, initials and time of that row. The row disappears from your phone at once and for good, also if the report could not be sent. We check every report within a day and may change the initials to KID, remove the time, or bar that device from the world board. A report is deleted 30 days after it was made, once we have dealt with it.

Switching it off and erasing. Tap the gear to open SETTINGS. WORLD BOARD: OFF stops all sending about you at once (only a report you make or an erase still sends your id); what is already online stays until you erase it. ERASE MY ONLINE TIMES deletes your times and counts on every list, any turned-down times of yours in the private log, the reports you made and the reports about you, switches the world board off, and forgets the id on your phone (your own times stay on your phone). The lists show the change within minutes. After an erase we keep only a decision we made about that id, if there is one (that it is barred, or that its initials were changed to KID), so that it cannot come back.

Deleted is not gone at once. Cloudflare's database keeps a point-in-time history of itself (Cloudflare calls it Time Travel: always on, 7 days on the free plan, 30 on the paid one), which we, as the account holder, can restore. So an erased or removed time, and a deleted day's network codes with their key, can still be brought back from that history for up to 7 days (30 on the paid plan).

5. What we use it for

Only for these: to run the world board (your place, the world lists with their tries and crashes, and checking reports), to find out where the game is too hard or broken, and to fix errors and slow frame rates. We do not sell it, do not share it with advertisers, do not build profiles, and do not use it to track you across other companies' apps or websites.

6. How long it is kept, and how to have it deleted

  • On your phone: until you delete it in the game (DELETE MINE for your voice, ANALYTICS: OFF for the analytics id, ERASE MY ONLINE TIMES for the world board id) or delete the game. Your answers to the game's questions are kept on your phone too, so the game does not ask again.
  • Analytics: GameAnalytics keeps the events under its own retention rules (see its policy below). Because the id is random and we never learn who you are, we cannot look up "your" events by name. Switching analytics off erases the id on your phone; you can also write to us or to GameAnalytics.
  • The world board: your best time stays on its list until you beat it or it is deleted, and your counts until they are deleted. ERASE MY ONLINE TIMES (section 4) deletes them at once. It works from the phone that sent them, because only that phone has the id: if you have deleted the game since, email gdenns@gmail.com with the level, the traffic mode, the ride, your initials and your time (that is how we find the line; we do not know who you are). Deleting and reinstalling the game gives your phone a new random id; the old time stays on the list until you ask us to remove it. Reports, and turned-down times in the private log (30 days at most), are kept as section 4 says. Erased data can still be restored from our host's history for up to 7 days (30 on its paid plan), as section 4 says.

We answer deletion requests within 30 days.

8. Your choices

  • Analytics is your choice: the game asks before it sends anything, and you can change your answer at any time (the gear opens SETTINGS; choose ANALYTICS: ON or OFF).
  • The world board is your choice too: the game asks before it sends anything about you. In SETTINGS, WORLD BOARD: ON or OFF changes your answer and ERASE MY ONLINE TIMES deletes what is online. Runs with changed gameplay settings are never sent. Rude initials or an impossible time on a world list: tap its flag, then REPORT, and it is gone from your phone at once.
  • The microphone is asked for only when you tap RECORD in the Scream Booth. You can say no; the game works without it, and you can take the permission back in the phone's Settings at any time.
  • Depending on where you live (for example the EU, the UK or California) you may have legal rights to see, correct or delete data about you, or to object to its use. Write to gdenns@gmail.com and we will help. You can also complain to your local data-protection authority.

9. Children

The game is a cartoon with slapstick crashes and is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. The only identifiers involved are the random ids described above, and they are used only to run the game's own features (the world board) and to understand how the game performs; never for ads or profiles. The world lists show three initials, never a name, and initials made of rude words are replaced. If you are a parent and believe your child has sent us information, write to gdenns@gmail.com and we will delete it.

10. Changes

If the game starts doing something new with data (for example ads or purchases), this page is updated first and the date at the top changes.
